SHA-1 Collision Attack Timeline & Alipay Impact Analysis
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

2005 — Theoretical Break (Wang et al.)
  Xiaoyun Wang demonstrated theoretical collision attack on SHA-1
  with complexity 2^69, far below birthday bound of 2^80.
  Impact: Academic alarm, no practical exploitation yet.

2011 — NIST Deprecation
  NIST officially deprecated SHA-1 for digital signatures.
  Impact: Government systems began migration to SHA-256.

2015 — Freestart Collision (Stevens et al.)
  First freestart collision in full SHA-1 compression function.
  Complexity: 2^57.5 (~10 days on 64-GPU cluster).
  Impact: Proved practical attacks imminent.

2017 — SHAttered (Stevens, Bursztein et al.)
  Google/CWI produced first identical-prefix collision.
  Two PDFs with same SHA-1 but different content.
  Cost: 6,500 CPU-years + 110 GPU-years ≈ $110,000 on GCP.
  Impact: Major browsers/CAs stopped accepting SHA-1 certificates.
  → Alipay cert (issued 2009, SHA-1 signed) became theoretically forgeable.

2019 — Chosen-Prefix Collision Breakthrough
  Leurent & Peyrin announced chosen-prefix collision on SHA-1.
  Complexity: 2^63.4 SHA-1 calls.
  Impact: Any two different prefixes can be made to collide.

2020 — SHA-1 is a Shambles (Leurent & Peyrin)
  Practical chosen-prefix collision demonstrated.
  Created two PGP keys with same SHA-1 certification.
  Cost: $45,000 on GPU cluster.
  Impact: PGP/GnuPG, Git, and certificate systems all affected.
  → Alipay cert forgery now practically feasible for well-funded attacker.

2023 — Cost Reduction
  GPU prices dropped significantly (H100 availability).
  Estimated chosen-prefix collision cost: ~$11,000.
  → Alipay cert forgery within reach of organized crime.

2026 — Current State (Estimated)
  Further hardware improvements + algorithmic optimizations.
  Estimated cost: $5,000-$8,000 for chosen-prefix collision.
  → Alipay cert forgery feasible for individual attackers.
  → Defense: APK Signature Scheme v2/v3 (SHA-256) is essential.

Key Insight for Alipay
══════════════════════
Alipay's APK signing certificate was issued in 2009 using
md5WithRSAEncryption with RSA-1024. While the MD5 vulnerability
(poc-01) is the most immediate threat, SHA-1 collision represents
a secondary attack vector:

1. Android's cert fingerprint matching historically used SHA-1
2. Some cert pinning implementations still reference SHA-1
3. Legacy v1 signature scheme relies on SHA-1 chain validation

The cost of forging a SHA-1-equivalent certificate has dropped from
$110,000 (2017) to an estimated $5,000-$8,000 (2026), making this
attack increasingly accessible.

Recommendation: All APK signature verification must use SHA-256+
fingerprints. SHA-1 fingerprints should be considered advisory only.
